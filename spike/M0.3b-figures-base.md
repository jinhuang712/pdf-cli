# M0.3b — Finding figures with Base only (PDFKit + Core Graphics, no ML model)

Question: Apple's document recognizer (M0.3) finds paragraphs, tables and lists but not figures. Can the "Base" engines alone — PDFKit text plus the drawing operators in the content stream, read with Core Graphics `CGPDFScanner` — find figures, vector charts and diagrams included, and pair them with their captions?

## Answer

**Yes, on born-digital arXiv papers.** On the 116 pages checked by eye, Base found and correctly boxed every real figure, 54 of 54, and reported no false figures. That means 100% precision and 100% recall on this sample. Figure boxes cover the plot or diagram including its axis labels, legends and sub-figure labels, and each one is paired with the right "Figure N" caption.

Time per page (text, content-stream scan, clustering and pairing; overlay rendering not included): median 8.9 ms (pages 1–6) and 8.1 ms (pages 7–12), max 25 ms. That is about 80 times faster than running Apple's recognizer on a 200 dpi render (median 670 ms in M0.3).

So for papers like these, an ML layout model is **not** needed to find figures. Keep these limits in mind:

- **Pages checked.** 116 pages from the 10 arXiv PDFs in `testdata/smoke/`: pages 1–6 of each (60 pages) and pages 7–12 of each where they exist (56 pages; the papers with 11, 11 and 10 pages stop early). The scanned NACA report was not tested: it has no drawing operators, only page images, so this method does not apply to it.
- **Tuning.** I tuned the heuristics on pages 1–6 and used pages 7–12 as a check set. The check set is not fully clean, though: two fixes came from looking at check-set pages. Before those fixes it scored 26 of 27; the one failure was arxiv-2609.06731 p11, where only the top panel of Fig 4's five-panel stack was boxed. The second fix stopped a "Table 3" caption that sits right under its table from being dropped (arxiv-2608.24191 p7); that fix changed the tables found, not the figure count.
- **Sample.** All 10 papers are recent LaTeX arXiv papers (9 two-column, 1 single-column). Every figure has a "Figure N:"/"Figure N." caption next to it. Other publishers, Word-made PDFs, captions without a number, or figures drawn as text (ASCII art, tables used as figures) were not tested.
- **Tables.** Tables are found by their horizontal rules and reported separately. I did not score them; the overlays show at least one table box that stretches too far (arxiv-2609.23257 p12, Table 9 runs up over the equations above it).

### What goes wrong (none of it produced a false or missed figure here)

1. **Framed text boxes show up as "unpaired clusters".** Coloured or framed boxes — "Takeaway" callouts (arxiv-2608.24191 p6–p9), prompt listings in appendices (arxiv-2608.16011 p11–12, arxiv-2608.22898 p11, arxiv-2608.23474 p12), table cell shading (arxiv-2609.06731 p7, p10, p12) and a title logo (arxiv-2608.26848 p1) — are drawings with no caption. They are listed as unpaired clusters, never as figures. A consumer that treats unpaired clusters as "figure without caption" would pick up these 17 false positives across 12 pages.
2. **In-text references at a line start.** "Table 8." at the start of a body line (arxiv-2609.23257 p7) still looks like a caption and is reported as an unpaired caption. A filter drops a match whose line above is a wide body line that does not end a sentence; this one still got through (I did not dig into why). Before that filter, "… is illustrated in" followed by a line starting "Figure 1. Full exclusion …" (arxiv-2608.24191 p5) was a second, unpaired "Figure 1" caption on the page. A real caption that follows a body line which happens not to end a sentence would be wrongly dropped by the same filter; I saw no such case.
3. **Boxes that are a bit too tight or need text to bridge panels.** Panel titles printed more than 8 pt above the plot area get left out (arxiv-2608.22898 p8, Fig 6 — "(a) Both Correct" and "(b) Both Incorrect"; counted as correct). Stacked panels separated by more than 24 pt only join because the sub-figure labels between them ("(a) …", "(b) …") are absorbed as text; without that, only one panel was boxed (arxiv-2609.06731 p11, before the fix).

## Per-page results

"Real figures" is my count by eye of charts, diagrams and photos (a multi-panel figure with one caption counts once). Algorithms, tables, framed text boxes and logos are not figures. "Found and correctly boxed" means the box covers the figure, its caption is the right one, and there is little empty space. "ms" is the measured time for that page.

## How the method works

Command: `pdf spike figures <pdf> [--pages 1-4] [--overlay <dir>]` (Sources/pdf/SpikeFigures.swift). It prints one JSON object per page: `figures` and `tables` (number, the first 120 characters of the caption, `captionBox`, `box`, and how many path and image marks the box covers), `unpairedClusters`, `unpairedCaptions`, `ms`, plus counts of marks, rules and dropped marks. Boxes are `[x, y, width, height]` as page fractions, with y measured from the top of the crop box. `--overlay` writes a 150 dpi PNG per page: figures red, tables blue, captions green, unpaired clusters dashed orange, unpaired captions solid orange. Setting `SPIKE_DEBUG=1` prints clusters, rules and text lines to stderr.

1. **Text lines.** `page.string` is split at line breaks, and each line's box comes from `page.selection(for: NSRange)` → `bounds(for: page)`.
2. **Captions.** A line counts as a caption when it starts with `Figure|FIGURE|Fig.|FIG.|Table|TABLE`, then a number (optionally a letter prefix such as A1), an optional sub-figure letter, and an optional `:` `.` `|`. Two filters remove in-text references:
   - When there is no punctuation after the number, the next word must be capitalized and there must be a gap above the line.
   - Any match is dropped when the line just above it (found by position, not text order) is a wide body line that does not end a sentence and no drawn rule sits between the two lines.

   The caption grows into its paragraph line by line. It takes the next line just below that overlaps it horizontally, or a fragment on the same row (PDFKit splits lines at inline math). It stops at another caption, at a drawn mark of 25 pt or wider between lines (a table rule), or, under a full-width caption, at a one-column line with a partner on the same row in the other column.
3. **Drawing marks.** `CGPDFScanner` runs over the page content stream with callbacks for `q Q cm` (CTM stack), `m l c v y re h` (path bbox under the CTM; curve control points included, so boxes are conservative), `f F f* S s B B* b b*` (paint → record), `n` (discard), `W W*` (rectangular clip, intersected into the graphics state), `sh` (records the current clip), `Do` and `EI`. For an image `Do`, the mark is the unit square under the CTM. For a form `Do`, the scanner recurses into the form's stream with `/Matrix` applied and `/BBox` used as a clip (depth limit 8). The form's own BBox is not recorded, so a form that wraps the whole page does not become one big mark. Every mark is clipped to the current clip and the crop box.
4. **Filters.**
   - Marks covering more than 50% of the page are dropped (backgrounds).
   - Path marks at most 2 pt thick and at least 10 pt long are **rules**: they do not start clusters. They join a cluster they touch (axes, grid lines), and table detection uses them.
   - Path marks under 0.5 pt in both directions are dropped.
   - Clusters whose longer side is under 15 pt, or whose area is under 300 pt², are dropped (glyph-like paths, bullets, inline legend squares).
5. **Clusters.** Marks whose boxes come within 8 pt of each other are merged repeatedly; touching rules are then added and the clusters merged again.
6. **Tables first.** From a "Table" caption, horizontal rules (wider than 20 pt) in the caption's column are chained in the direction of the closer first rule: below the caption (most styles) or above it (ACL style). The first rule must be within 30 pt. Chaining stops at another caption or a gap larger than 30% of the page height. Clusters that lie at least 60% inside the table box (cell shading) are claimed by the table.
7. **Figures.** A caption's band is its column, or the whole page when the caption crosses the middle (5% margin). The figure is the nearest unused cluster above the caption in that band (within 15% of the page height, with no other caption in between); if there is none above, the nearest one below. The figure then grows, in alternating rounds:
   - with other clusters in the band less than 24 pt away vertically, or on the same rows (sub-figures side by side);
   - with short text lines (under a third of the page width) within 8 pt (axis labels, legends, panel labels).

   Finally, text lines between the drawing and its caption that fit within the figure's width (sub-figure captions) are added.
8. Clusters left over are reported as `unpairedClusters`, and captions without a match as `unpairedCaptions`.

## Known limits

- **Only drawings are seen.** A figure built only from text (a table used as a figure, ASCII art) has no marks, so it would be reported as an unpaired caption. Scans have one page-sized image, which the 50% filter drops, so nothing is found on them.
- **Captions are required to pair.** A figure with no "Figure N" caption, or with an unusual label ("Fig. S3", "Abbildung 2", "Chart 1"), comes out only as an unpaired cluster. Framed text boxes come out the same way, so the two cannot be told apart without another signal.
- **Fixed thresholds in points.** The 8 pt cluster gap, 24 pt figure gap, 15% search distance and 2 pt rule thickness were tuned on 10-pt, two-column LaTeX papers. Posters, slides or dense single-column layouts may need other values.
- **Rectangular clip only.** Clipping paths are reduced to their bounding box, and soft masks and transparency groups are ignored, so a mark that is clipped out but whose bbox overlaps the page still counts.
- **Type 3 glyph procedures are not scanned**, and `sh` without a clip is skipped.
- **Column model.** Columns are taken as the left and right halves of the page. Three-column layouts and wrapped figures (`wrapfigure`) were not tested.
- **Table boxes are rough.** They are rule chains only: borderless tables are missed, and a chain can run past the table when rules from nearby content line up (arxiv-2609.23257 p12).
