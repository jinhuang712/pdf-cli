# M1.9 — `pdf crop` for pages and regions

- **Built:** 2026-10-15 in `a8ad1d4`, with review fixes in the commit after it.
- **Written:** 2026-10-15, on the task's branch.

## What was built

- `pdf crop <pdf> --page N`, with `--bbox x,y,width,height` for a region and `--grid` for lines labelled in page fractions. It prints the image's absolute path, its size and its image tokens; `--json` gives the same.
- A worker draws the image (`__worker crop`), in a slot of the shared pool, within the page's time and memory limits.
- Images fit Claude's standard limits: 1,568 px on the long edge and 1,568 tokens of 28×28 px. A region is enlarged up to 6 times.
- A region is in fractions of the page as stored, before its rotation, the frame block positions and failure blocks use; the image comes out upright, with comments and highlights as a reader sees them.
- Images live in `crops/` in the cache folder, one folder per version of a file, for 7 days. The same crop within them answers from the folder, and `pdf gc` removes older ones.
- `--password-stdin` opens a locked PDF, passing the password to the worker in its environment.
- Found while building: a command that asked the reader for no pages, to get only the index, sent the runner `--pages=`, which the argument parser refused, so the read failed after three tries. Fixed in Reader: the empty list is left out.

## Review

The reviewer built the commit and ran both test scripts. It checked the geometry on PDFs it made with a crop box away from the page's origin and every rotation: a block's position from `pdf read --json` crops exactly that block, upright, and the grid's labels sit on the right fractions. It confirmed the token count against Claude's vision documentation. It found these:

| # | Finding | Verdict | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Crops run together removed each other's folders: a crop's folder is empty while it waits for its worker, and another crop's cleanup removed empty folders. 9 of 10 crops of the samples run together failed with "the image could not be written". | Confirmed, high | Fixed: a folder is removed only after an hour empty, and the worker makes it again before it writes. An end-to-end check crops 6 new files at once. |
| 2 | The worker got the region rounded to the nearest hundredth: a region thinner than half a hundredth, or one at the page's far edge, became nothing, and the worker drew the whole page and saved it under the region's name. Rounding also cut up to 4 pt off a block's edge. | Confirmed, high | Fixed: regions round outward, and the region drawn is the region named; a region that does not parse fails instead of drawing the page. Crop commands in failure blocks round outward too. Tests in `aRegionRoundsOutwardToHundredths` and an end-to-end check of a hairline region. |
| 3 | The finished image replaced the old one in two steps, so two crops of one region finishing together could fail, with an error that skipped the JSON form. | Likely | Fixed: `rename(2)` replaces it at once, and every failure prints as the crop's own error, in JSON too. |
| 4 | A locked PDF's message named `pdf add --password`, which does not help a crop. | Likely, low | Fixed: it names `pdf crop … --password-stdin`. |
| 5 | Smaller: a crop stopped by a signal left its partial image for 7 days; the worker's output was read only after it exited; the grid's label boxes hid page text at the edges. | Likely, low | Fixed: partial images go after an hour, the output is read while the worker runs, and the label boxes are lighter. A page 14,400 pt long cropped whole comes out 2 px tall, which the size cap makes inherent; `--bbox` crops part of it. |

## Tests

127 unit tests passed, 4 of them new, one of them run at each of four rotations: a region crops the same spot of the page at 0°, 90°, 180° and 270°, a crop fits Claude's limits, a region rounds outward, and a region past the page's edge is cut there. All end-to-end checks passed, 12 of them new, among them a crop command taken from a failure block, a page that hangs while drawn, a locked PDF with and without its password, a hairline region, and 6 crops at once.
