# M2.5 — Reading order across columns, with running headers set aside

- **Built:** 2026-10-17, on the task's branch.
- **Written:** 2026-10-17, on the task's branch. Under the user's rule of 2026-10-17, tests are light until the final wrap-up: unit and end-to-end tests, one quick review.

## What was built

**Columns.** PDFKit's order of a page's lines reads well on most pages, but on some it goes from one column to the other line by line. Below the figure on p.5 of the smoke set's arxiv-2608.24191.pdf, each paragraph came out as one-line blocks of both columns in turn, 41 blocks where the page holds 12. The parse now keeps PDFKit's order and moves only what is clearly a band of two columns of running text (`ReadingOrder`):

- The gutter is the place in the middle half of the text that the fewest lines cross. A quarter of the lines or more crossing it means one column, and nothing moves.
- A line across both columns, reaching a quarter of the way into each, such as a title or a full-width caption, parts the page into bands.
- A band moves when each side holds two lines or more that fill 80% of its column, and neither side is itself two such columns, as on a page of three. Its left column goes before its right one, right before left in Hebrew and Arabic, each keeping PDFKit's order of its lines. The band goes out where PDFKit first reaches one of its full lines, the bands from the top down.
- Every other line keeps its place:
  - a short line between the columns' edges, such as a page number;
  - what stands above the columns, parted from them by a blank a line high, such as a grid of authors;
  - lines across both columns, with a caption's last line right below them, short or set at the caption's size.
- Lines over three times as tall as most, such as arXiv's stamp set sideways, take no part in finding the columns.
- The reading order tells the grouping where each column starts a block, so the left column's first line under a full-width caption no longer joins the caption.
- Parser version 14: every page is parsed again on its next read.

**Running headers, footers, page numbers and publisher stamps** (`Furniture`) are judged when a page is read, against the pages within 4 of it parsed so far. The candidates are blocks that:

- sit in the page's top or bottom 12% as its outermost text there, within 2.5% of its highest or lowest block, or in its outer 10% at a side;
- hold three lines at most and no text a reader cannot see;
- are among the 4 nearest each edge.

A candidate is furniture when it:

- repeats at the same edge of a page nearby, with the same letters and digits once a page number at either end is dropped, or nine in ten alike for twenty or more, as OCR misreads the NACA scan's headers. A short one, under 15, must repeat on two pages.
- or is a page number alone that agrees with the page's own number, printed or by position, or with a page number nearby: "12", "- 12 -", "Page 12 of 30", "11841" or "xii". A lowercase roman numeral, well formed. As the page's highest or lowest block it may stand within 15% of the edge, as an old TeX paper sets its number 87.6% down.
- or is arXiv's stamp in the side margin.

- `pdf read` leaves them out, keeps their block numbers, and counts them in the summary line: `running headers and footers: 4 set aside (show: --headers)`. Each counts where it stands, so a read cut by the budget, a section's end or `--from` counts what it passed.
- `--headers` shows them in place, as `¶9 [footer] 2`. `--json` gives each one's kind in `status` and the count in `setAside.runningHeaders`.
- An annotation on one still goes out.
- `pdf info` lists each once, with its kind and page count.
- Headings in a file without bookmarks leave them out, in place of the top 7.5% and bottom 5% bands, which dropped real headings set high.

**Not in this task:**
- The check that flags an uncertain reading order moved to the final wrap-up, with M2.12's tuning: a flag needs a threshold tuned on the open test set.
- A scanned page read by OCR keeps the recognizer's order.

## Limits

- Two columns per band. A page of three columns keeps PDFKit's order, and so does a caption across the page, which on arxiv-2608.26848.pdf p.3 PDFKit reads after the columns below it.
- A table's header row repeated at the top of each page of a long table counts as a running header: one cell of the census report's tables, "Margin of error² (±)", on 14 pages.
- While a file parses, a header can show until its neighbours are parsed, and the summary line says the read is provisional. A neighbour parsed between the judging and the end of the read can leave a read that looks final with a header judged without it (the review, from the code; not reproduced).
- A line PDFKit splits at the gutter is moved in two pieces: harmless where both are furniture, as a footer on the Moroccan report's p.13.
- A stamp set lower than the page's footer, as "XSL•FO RenderX" on a JMIR paper, keeps the footer from counting as outermost: the footer stays in the text.
- `pdf search` still finds running headers, one hit a page: `TODO.md`.
  - 2026-10-25: M2.22 changed this: `pdf search` leaves running headers out (`reports/M2.22-search-headers.md`).
- Line numbers in a review copy's margin are not set aside.

## Tests

- **Unit:**
  - `ReadingOrderTests`, 11 tests:
    - columns in turn go together, and columns read in order stay;
    - a caption parts the bands, and its breaks hold, and its last line stays with it;
    - one column, and a table's cells, stay;
    - right-to-left columns start on the right;
    - a page number in the gutter and authors above the columns keep their place;
    - a stamp in the margin moves nothing ahead;
    - arxiv-2608.24191.pdf p.5 reads its paragraph whole.
  - `FurnitureTests`, 12 tests:
    - a repeated header and a page number are set aside;
    - footnotes and headings that do not repeat stay;
    - page numbers in four forms, one 87.6% down and a journal's that agree, are found;
    - tick labels, a cover's year, "MIX", "Civil" and a number that agrees with no page are no page numbers;
    - arXiv's stamp is found in the margin, and a reference that starts "arXiv:" at the top is not;
    - a misread header matches;
    - a box's heading below the running header stays;
    - "Table B-1." to "B-3." and "ENDNOTES" on two pages stay;
    - a block with hidden text stays;
    - a thousand blocks in a margin give at most 4 candidates;
    - `pdf read` sets them aside, and `--headers` shows them;
    - each counts where it stands: a page of nothing else, above a section's end, before a budget's cut.
  - 200 unit tests in all.
- **Safety set:** `hidden-in-page-number.pdf`, white words planted after a page number: G16, 71 of 71.

  - p.5 of arxiv-2608.24191.pdf reads its left column's paragraph whole;
  - the page numbers of arxiv-2608.16011.pdf p.2–5 are set aside, counted, and shown with `--headers`.
